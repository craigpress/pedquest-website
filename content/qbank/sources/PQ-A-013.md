## Where each number came from
All from the Goenka 2018 abstract, quoted with denominators rather than bare percentages:
- Cohort: 562 seizures from 58 patients, ages 5 to 64 years, mean 36; 125 focal, 187 secondarily generalized, 250 generalized; recorded in the ICU or EMU between July 2016 and January 2017.
- "Overall sensitivities of the QEEG spectrograms for detecting seizures ranged from 43% to 72%, with highest sensitivity (402/562, 72%) by the seizure detection trend."
- "The asymmetry spectrogram had the highest sensitivity for detecting focal seizures (117/125, 94%)."
- "The FFT spectrogram was most sensitive for detecting secondarily generalized seizures (158/187, 84%)."
- "The seizure detection trend was the most sensitive for generalized onset seizures (197/250, 79%)."

## Judgment calls
- **Population labelled honestly.** The study is a mixed adult and pediatric cohort (mean age 36 y). Both the explanation and a key_point state this explicitly and warn against direct pediatric extrapolation, per STYLE_GUIDE section 2.
- The item is set in an EMU rather than a PICU because the source cohort included EMU recordings and because the presurgical scenario makes a single-trend choice a realistic workflow decision.
- **Vendor.** The source studied Persyst version 12 trends. The stem uses generic trend names; the vendor is not named because the teaching point (match the trend to the seizure type) generalises, and the citation carries the vendor detail.
- The panel is rendered with panel_labels hidden so that the item cannot be answered by reading a label off the image.
