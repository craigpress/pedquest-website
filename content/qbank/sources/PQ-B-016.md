## Where each number came from
Fung 2020. Population: prospective observational cohort of 719 consecutive critically ill children with encephalopathy; multistate survival model; registered NCT03419260.
- Electrographic seizures in 184 children (26 %).
- Baseline clinical risk factors: age and prior clinically evident seizures. Emergent EEG risk factors: epileptiform discharges and ictal-interictal continuum patterns.
- Duration to reach a residual electrographic seizure risk below 5 %: (1) 6 hours if 1 year or older without prior seizures or EEG risk factors; (2) 1 day if under 1 year without prior seizures or EEG risks; (3) 1 day if 1 year or older with either prior seizures or EEG risks; (4) 2 days if 1 year or older with both; (5) 2 days if under 1 year without prior seizures but with EEG risks; (6) 2.5 days if under 1 year with prior seizures regardless of EEG risks.
- For a residual risk below 2 %, the same durations applied except for the groups without prior seizures or EEG risk factors, which required 1.5 days (under 1 year) and 1 day (1 year or older).

Abend 2015, cited as supporting only: 48 hours of monitoring would be worth its cost if identification and management of electrographic status epilepticus improved outcomes by 7 % or more; 24 hours if by as little as 3 %. No figure from this paper appears in the stem or explanation.

## Judgment calls
- The vignette states all four risk factors explicitly (age 5 years, no prior clinically evident seizure, no epileptiform discharges, no ictal-interictal continuum patterns) so that exactly one row of the model applies.
- Option B (12 hours) is not a decision point in the model at all; it is included because it is the duration clinicians most often guess.
- The item asks for the 5 % threshold, which is the model's headline output; the 2 % variant is described in the explanation so a reader can see that this child's group is one of the two for which the two thresholds differ.
