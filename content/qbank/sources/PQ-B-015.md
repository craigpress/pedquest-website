## Where each number came from
Appavu 2022 (Epilepsy Res). Population: 61 surviving children who underwent multimodality neurologic monitoring after traumatic brain injury, including continuous EEG and intracranial pressure monitoring; outcome post-traumatic epilepsy, defined as unprovoked seizures 2 months after injury or a continued need for antiseizure medication at 12 months.
- Post-traumatic epilepsy in 10 of 61 (16.4 %).
- Early post-traumatic seizures within 7 days: OR 7.58 (95 % CI 1.81-39.68).
- Seizures occurring 24-168 h after injury: OR 21.47 (4.18-110.38). Seizures within the first 24 h: not significantly associated.
- Among children with early seizures, increased time in seizure on surface EEG: OR 7.28 (2.05-73.14).
- Epileptiform discharges OR 8.06 (1.85-35.17); abnormal sleep spindles OR 4.88 (1.18-20.00); increased intracranial pressure OR 1.25 (1.02-1.52); increased CT Marshall score OR 1.76 (1.13-2.74).

Appavu 2024 (J Clin Neurophysiol), used only in the supporting reference list: 72 children, 146 seizures in 19 patients; intracranial pressure negatively associated with ictal spectral edge frequency (standardized coefficient -0.12, 99 % credible interval -0.21 to -0.04).

## Judgment calls
- Option E is wrong in a specific, informative way: raised intracranial pressure was itself an independent predictor, so "only when intracranial pressure is normal" inverts the finding.
- The vignette places the seizures on the second day (roughly 30-54 h) so the recording sits squarely inside the 24-168 h window that carries the association.
- The confidence intervals extend to 110.38 and 73.14 because the cohort has 61 patients and 10 events. The explanation reports them in full rather than quoting point estimates alone.
- Vaewpanich 2016 is cited as supporting because its qualitative findings (nonreactive background, burst suppression, absent sleep architecture) align, but with only 16 children no number from it is used.
