## Where each definition came from
All read verbatim from the ACNS 2021 full text (PMC8135051), section B, Continuity:
- "Nearly Continuous: continuous, but with occasional (1-9% of the record) periods of attenuation or suppression lasting >=1 second."
- "Attenuation: periods of lower voltage are >=10 uV but <50% of the higher voltage background."
- "Suppression: periods of lower voltage are <10 uV."
- "Discontinuous: A pattern of attenuation/suppression alternating with higher voltage activity, with 10% to 49% of the record consisting of attenuation or suppression."
- "Burst attenuation/Burst suppression: ... with 50% to 99% of the record consisting of attenuation ... or suppression."
- "NOTE: This voltage cutoff, as with other voltages, differs from the ACNS neonatal terminology."
The summary-of-changes table in the same paper confirms the 2021 revisions quoted in the rationales: nearly continuous changed from at most 10 percent to 1 to 9 percent; burst suppression from more than 50 percent to 50 to 99 percent; suppression/attenuation from the entirety of the record to more than 99 percent.

## Judgment calls
- **The mapping from suppression ratio to ACNS category is only valid when the trend threshold is 10 uV**, so the vignette states that the technologist set it there and the explanation makes the caveat explicit. Without that sentence the item would be scientifically wrong, since the Benedetti worked example uses a 5 uV threshold.
- The five options are ordered by ascending percentage, per STYLE_GUIDE section 1 (numeric ascending).
- Option A was lengthened from the single word "Continuous" to keep option lengths homogeneous and remove a length cue.
- The stem states no sedative infusion so the reader is not invited to answer a pharmacology question instead.
