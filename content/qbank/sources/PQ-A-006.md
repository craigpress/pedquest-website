## Where each fact came from
- "78% of all neonatal seizures appeared in the C3 to C4 channel"; "94% of the conventional electroencephalograms had at least 1 neonatal seizure visible in C3 to C4"; "conceptional ages were 34 to 50 weeks"; "851 neonatal seizures from 125 conventional electroencephalograms"; "seizures were briefer and lower in amplitude in C3 to C4"; and the multivariate predictors of detection (appearance in C3 to C4, duration, amplitude, count per hour, reader experience) all come from the Shellhaas 2007 abstract.
- "121 seizure patterns with a mean duration of 58 s were identified, 68% of which occurred over the centrotemporal region" — Bourez-Swart 2009 abstract.
- "abnormalities occurring far from the electrodes may not be detected" — Benedetti 2023, Amplitude-Focused Trends, item 1, PMC10033183.

## Judgment calls
- The vignette explicitly rules out hypothermia, artifact and poor impedances so that distractors B, D and E are excluded from the stem alone. Without those clauses the item would have more than one defensible answer.
- The image.spec models the events as a seizure_cluster with onset_region right_frontal and spread none, with the aEEG derived from C3-C4, so the renderer deterministically produces a normal-looking aEEG alongside an annotation strip marking the reference seizures. That is what makes the answer visible rather than merely asserted.
- Shellhaas 2007 studied 34 to 50 weeks conceptional age; the vignette uses a term neonate on day of life 2, inside that range.
