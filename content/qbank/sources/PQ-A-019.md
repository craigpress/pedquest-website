## Where each number came from
Topjian 2015 abstract, verbatim:
- 39 critical care providers, being 20 critical care physicians (12 attendings and 8 fellows) and 19 critical care nurses; 100 CDSA images of which 30 percent displayed seizures, presented in duplicate and in random order after a 30-minute tutorial.
- "sensitivity of 70% (95% CI, 67-73%), specificity of 68% (95% CI, 67-70%), positive predictive value of 46%, and negative predictive value of 86%."
- "The sensitivity of color density spectral array detection of status epilepticus was 72% (95% CI, 69-74%)."

Du Pont-Thibodeau 2017 abstract, verbatim:
- 23 critical care providers; 100 aEEG images and 100 combined aEEG plus CDSA images.
- aEEG: "sensitivity was 77% (95% CI, 73%-80%), specificity of 65% (95% CI, 62%-67%), negative predictive value of 88% (95% CI, 86%-90%), and positive predictive value of 46% (95% CI, 43%-49%)."
- aEEG plus CDSA: "sensitivity was 77% (95% CI, 74%-81%), specificity of 68% (95% CI, 66%-71%), negative predictive value of 89% (95% CI, 87%-90%), and positive predictive value of 49% (95% CI, 46%-52%)."
- Status epilepticus sensitivity 77 percent with aEEG and 75 percent with aEEG plus CDSA.
- "The addition of color density spectral array to amplitude-integrated electroencephalography did not improve seizure detection. However, 87% of critical care medicine providers qualitatively felt that combining both modalities increased their ability to detect seizures."

## Judgment calls
- **Both studies were image-classification exercises, not real-time bedside trials.** The explanation says so explicitly, because the item would otherwise invite over-generalisation.
- The ranges in the options (70 to 77 percent sensitivity, 86 to 89 percent NPV, 46 to 49 percent PPV, 65 to 68 percent specificity) are the spans across the two studies, not new pooled estimates. Every endpoint of every span is a published figure.
- Distractor D inverts the counterintuitive true finding that adding CDSA did not improve detection; the confidence result is reported in the rationale so the learner sees both halves.
- The image places a brief, low-amplitude, single-chain seizure in the raw page at a time the screen called negative, which is precisely the seizure profile the literature reports trends miss.
