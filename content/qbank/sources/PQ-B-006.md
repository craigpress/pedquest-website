## Where each number came from
Ostendorf 2016. Population: non-neonatal children with an arrest lasting more than 2 minutes, monitored within 72 h of return of spontaneous circulation; multivariable analysis of 73 children.
- Arrest duration under 20 minutes **or** continuous background activity within 12 hours of return of spontaneous circulation was associated with good short-term neurologic outcome.
- Change in background score over time and electroencephalographic data collected after the initial hour were **not** associated with outcome.
Abend 2018. Population: 89 consecutive children after cardiac arrest, 453 EEG segments scored in 12-hour epochs to 72 h.
- Two uncorrelated factors, background and intermittent.
- Factor scores did not change across epochs (background coefficient 0.18, p = 0.52; intermittent -0.003, p = 0.70); heat maps showed individual change, mostly in children with medium initial scores.

## Judgment calls
- The Ostendorf abstract's wording ("data collected after the initial hour were not associated with outcome") is reproduced faithfully; option D is phrased as the end-of-monitoring score rather than restating the abstract verbatim.
- The item does not claim that serial EEG has no clinical value. The rationales say the association was not demonstrated in this cohort, and the explanation reports that some individual children did shift.
- `eeg_page` rather than a trend panel was chosen so that continuity, the graded feature in question, is judged on raw EEG, which is where the cited scoring was performed.
