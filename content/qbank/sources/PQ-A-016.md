## Where each number came from
Shellhaas 2007 abstract, verbatim:
- "A total of 851 neonatal seizures from 125 conventional electroencephalograms were analyzed. The patients conceptional ages were 34 to 50 weeks."
- "94% of the conventional electroencephalograms had 1 or more neonatal seizure visible in C3 to C4, and 78% of all neonatal seizures appeared in the C3 to C4 channel."
- "Neonatologists identified seizures in 22% to 57% of the 125 records of neonatal seizure. They detected 12% to 38% of the 851 individual seizures." Six neonatologists of varying expertise.
- "Even among physicians who have extensive experience, many neonatal seizures are difficult to detect on an aEEG, especially when they are infrequent, brief, or of low amplitude."
Rakshasbhuvankar 2015 abstract, verbatim:
- Ten studies, patient sample 433.
- "When aEEG with raw trace was used, median sensitivity was 76% (range: 71-85), and specificity 85% (range: 39-96). When aEEG without raw trace was used, median sensitivity was 39% (range: 25-80) and specificity 95% (range: 50-100)."
- "aEEG cannot be recommended as the mainstay for diagnosis and management of neonatal seizures."

## Judgment calls
- **The four percentages are deliberately kept apart** because they are the item: 94 percent (records with any seizure visible in the channel), 78 percent (individual seizures present in the channel), 22 to 57 percent (records in which readers found a seizure) and 12 to 38 percent (individual seizures readers found). Every distractor is one of these numbers attached to the wrong denominator.
- The "roughly doubles" phrasing for raw-trace reading compares median 39 percent to median 76 percent, both quoted; no new number is invented.
- Shellhaas 2011 (ACNS neonatal cEEG guideline) is supporting only; no number is drawn from it, because its abstract is not available in PubMed and I did not read the full text.
