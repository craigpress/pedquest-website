## Where each number came from
Wagenman 2014. Population: 300 children with an acute neurologic condition and encephalopathy who underwent clinically indicated EEG monitoring; 137 were reported neurodevelopmentally normal before PICU admission; follow-up obtained for 60 of those 137 (44 %) at a median of 2.7 years.
- Among those followed: electrographic seizures 12 (20 %), electrographic status epilepticus 14 (23 %).
- Multivariable, adjusted for neurologic disorder category, EEG background category and age: ESE -> unfavorable extended paediatric Glasgow Outcome Scale category OR 6.36 (p = 0.01); Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory 23 points lower (p = 0.001); among those without a prior epilepsy diagnosis, subsequently diagnosed epilepsy OR 13.3 (p = 0.002).
- Electrographic seizures were not associated with worse outcomes.

## Judgment calls
- The question asks specifically about *subsequently diagnosed epilepsy* rather than which outcome is "most increased", to avoid comparing an odds ratio (6.36) with a point difference (23 PedsQL points).
- The explanation reports the follow-up fraction (60 of 137) because the imprecision is material to how the number should be used with a family.
- The stem's population is stated as previously typically developing, matching the subgroup in which the 13.3 odds ratio was derived.
