## Where each number came from
Naim 2021. Population: multicenter prospective cohort in which all postoperative neonates who underwent cardiopulmonary bypass also underwent continuous EEG.
- Electrographic seizures in 7.4 % (78 of 1053).
- Model predictors: gestational age, head circumference, single-ventricle defect, deep hypothermic circulatory arrest duration, cardiac arrest, nitric oxide, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, delayed sternal closure.
- Derivation cohort: c-statistic 0.77, Hosmer-Lemeshow p = 0.56, net benefit over monitoring all or none above a 2 % threshold probability in decision curve analysis.
- Validation cohort: good calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow p = 0.60) but poor discrimination (c-statistic 0.61) and **no net benefit** between threshold probabilities of 8 % and 18 %.
- The authors' stated conclusion: these findings support continuous EEG of all neonates after cardiopulmonary bypass.

Naim 2015 (supporting): seizures in 13 of 161 monitored neonates (8 %), electroencephalogram only in 11 of 13 (85 %), median onset 20 h after return to the intensive care unit.

## Judgment calls
- The correct answer restates the cited paper's own conclusion, so the item tests a guideline-level inference rather than a number.
- The derivation-cohort operating point (a cut point at which 236 of 737 neonates, 32 %, would not undergo monitoring, with negative predictive value 99 % and sensitivity 97 %) was verified but deliberately **not** used, because it is a derivation-cohort figure that the validation cohort did not support; quoting it would undercut the answer.
- Shellhaas 2011 is cited as supporting for the general recommendation to monitor high-risk neonates; no numeric claim is attributed to it, because its full text is not open access.
