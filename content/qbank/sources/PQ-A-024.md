## Where each criterion came from
ACNS 2021 full text (PMC8135051), read verbatim:
- Section D1, electrographic seizure: "Epileptiform discharges averaging >2.5 Hz for >=10 seconds (>25 discharges in 10 seconds), OR Any pattern with definite evolution as defined above and lasting >=10 seconds."
- Section D2, electrographic status epilepticus: "ESE is defined as an ESz for >=10 continuous minutes or for a total duration of >=20% of any 60-minute period of recording." The document adds that the 20 percent cutoff, lowered from the previous 50 percent, rests on expert consensus and on one study in critically ill children in whom the risk of neurological decline was significantly greater when the maximum hourly seizure burden exceeded 20 percent, with a similar cutoff identified in neonates with hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy.
- Section F, ictal-interictal continuum: "The IIC is a purely electrographic term that is not a diagnosis"; the three qualifying patterns, including "Any lateralized RDA averaging >1 Hz for at least 10 seconds (at least 10 waves in 10 seconds) with a plus modifier or fluctuation ... This includes any LRDA, BIRDA, UIRDA, and MfRDA, but not GRDA"; the requirement that it "Does not qualify as an ESz or ESE"; and that it "often warrants a diagnostic treatment trial, typically with a parenteral antiseizure medication".
- Section E: "technically all BIRDs are very brief because they are <10 seconds", which excludes distractor E.

## Judgment calls
- **The pediatric seizure-burden study behind the 20 percent cutoff is not cited here.** ACNS attributes the cutoff to a study in critically ill children, but that primary paper sits in the clinical_prognosis domain assigned to Writer B. The explanation therefore reports the cutoff as an ACNS definition and attributes the supporting evidence generically, rather than quoting a number from a paper I did not verify myself.
- The vignette states that the pattern does not evolve, has no superimposed fast activity or sharp waves (hence no plus modifier), and shows fluctuation. Those three clauses are what leave exactly one option correct.
- Distractor D is the incomplete-but-not-wrong naming answer, the realistic trainee error; its rationale explains why stopping at the name changes management.
