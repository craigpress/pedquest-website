## Where each fact came from
- The 80 percent worked example — Benedetti 2023, Amplitude-Focused Trends, item 3 (Suppression ratio), read verbatim from PMC10033183: "if during a 10-second epoch of EEG, eight of the seconds had maximum amplitudes < 5 uV, the percentage suppression would be 80%." The vignette reproduces that arithmetic exactly, so the answer is derivable from the stem alone and no reader has to recall the paper.
- "A running average is calculated with percentage suppression on the y-axis" — same paragraph.
- "particularly useful for ... pharmacologically induced burst suppression" and "the suppression ratio can be used to assess the degree of burst suppression and titrate anesthetic infusions to a specific target for SRSE and refractory intracranial hypertension management" — same paragraph plus the section Assessing Response to Antiseizure Medication and Infusion Titration.
- ACNS 2021 is cited only as the terminology authority for the term burst suppression. No number is taken from it in this item.

## Judgment calls
- **The 60 to 80 percent target band is framed as a target written by this team, not as a guideline number.** No cited source specifies a numeric suppression-ratio target for pediatric super-refractory status epilepticus, so presenting one as standard would violate STYLE_GUIDE section 2. The wording in the vignette is deliberate.
- The 5 microvolt threshold is presented as the configured trend setting, matching the worked example in the source, and the explanation states explicitly that the threshold is configurable.
- Distractor E converts the burst amplitude (90) into a percentage, testing amplitude-versus-duration confusion. Distractor A is the burst fraction, testing the inversion.
