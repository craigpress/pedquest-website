## Where each fact came from
- "rhythmic patterns such as patting and respiratory therapy artifact, and both can be mistaken for seizures if the raw EEG and video are not reviewed" — Benedetti 2023, Common qEEG Artifacts in the Pediatric ICU, PMC10033183.
- "susceptible to highlighting nonictal rhythmic patterns, including ... repetitive artifacts (respiratory treatments or patting)" — Benedetti 2023, Frequency-Focused Trends, item 4.
- Stewart 2010 abstract, verbatim: "The 27 EEG recordings contained 553 discrete seizures over 487 hours"; "The median sensitivity for seizure identification across all recordings was 83.3% using CDSA and 81.5% using aEEG"; "False-positive rates were generally very low, with misidentified seizures occurring once every 17-20 hours."

## Judgment calls
- **Stewart 2010 primary-use budget.** This is the second and last item in which Stewart is a primary reference; the other is PQ-A-011, as required by the assignment.
- **Answer region derives from the spec.** target_event index 1 is the seizure at onset_min 150.0; index 0 is the chest_pt artifact at 70.0. target_panel is rhythmicity_L.
- The chest physiotherapy annotation is drawn on the image because a bedside reader would have that information, per IMAGE_SPEC guidance on labelling a teaching distractor.
- The artifact is specified bilaterally (side all) with a fixed artifact_band_hz of 3.0 so the renderer produces a non-evolving band. That is what makes the discrimination visible rather than merely asserted in the stem.
- The false-positive figure is reported as about once every 17 to 20 hours, exactly as published and not rounded.
