## Where each number came from
Bourez-Swart 2009 abstract, verbatim:
- "Twelve infants with cEEG seizure patterns were investigated."
- "On cEEG, 121 seizure patterns with a mean duration of 58 s were identified, 68% of which occurred over the centrotemporal region."
- "The sensitivity of aEEG for the detection of seizure patterns was 30% (C.I.: 0.22-0.38) for single-channel aEEG and 39% (C.I.: 0.31-0.48) for multichannel aEEG."
- "Multichannel aEEG identified all patients with 1 seizure pattern (C.I.: 0.75-1.00), whereas single-channel aEEG (with C4-C3 as the optimal electrode derivation) identified all but one of the patients (C.I.: 0.66-0.99)."
- "Patient identification can be improved using multichannel recordings."

## Judgment calls
- **Seizure-level versus patient-level detection is the whole point of the item**, and the two are never blended into one figure. The distractors are built from the plausible ways a reader collapses them.
- The confidence intervals and the small selected sample (n = 12) are stated in the explanation, because a 0.75 to 1.00 interval on a patient-level claim is weak evidence and the item must not let a learner treat it as settled.
- The source title uses the word subclinical. STYLE_GUIDE section 2 discourages that term outside direct quotation, so the item consistently says electrographic seizure and the word survives only inside the verbatim citation title.
- Tsuchida 2013 is supporting; it supplies neonatal terminology framing and no number.
- The item explicitly refuses the claim that multichannel aEEG can measure seizure burden, because 39 percent sensitivity does not support it; the correct option is worded to say exactly that.
