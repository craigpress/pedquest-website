## Where each number came from
Thoresen 2010. Population: 74 infants recruited with CoolCap entry criteria, aEEG recorded for 72 h, Bayley Scales of Infant Development II at 18 months.
- Positive predictive value of an abnormal aEEG pattern at 3-6 h of age: 84 % normothermia, 59 % hypothermia.
- A moderately abnormal *voltage* background at 3-6 h did not predict outcome.
- Recovery time to a normal background pattern was the best predictor of poor outcome: 96.2 % hypothermia, 90.9 % normothermia.
- Never developing sleep-wake cycling always predicted poor outcome; time to cycling predicted better under hypothermia (88.5 %) than normothermia (63.6 %).
- Infants with good outcome normalized by 24 h under normothermia and by 48 h under hypothermia.

Hallberg 2010. Population: 23 infants treated with moderate hypothermia (33.5 C for 72 h), drawn from a birth cohort of 28,837; motor function assessed at 4 and 12 months.
- 10 of 15 infants with a severely abnormal burst-suppression pattern or worse at 6 h of age had a normal 1-year outcome.
- Only aEEG abnormalities persisting at and beyond 24 h were followed by poor neurological outcome; severe abnormalities were significantly predictive after 36 h.

## Judgment calls
- Option D ("stop cooling") is included because it is the clinically dangerous inference this literature exists to prevent; its rationale names it as the most harmful option rather than treating it as neutral.
- "48 hours" in the correct option is the Thoresen normalization time; the "24 h / 36 h" in the option C rationale is Hallberg. The two papers are kept separate in the explanation so each figure is traceable.
- Thoresen's percentages for "recovery time to normal pattern" and "time to SWC" are predictive accuracies as reported in the abstract. They are quoted as such and are **not** relabelled as sensitivity or specificity.
