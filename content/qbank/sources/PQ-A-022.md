## Where each definition came from
ACNS 2021 full text (PMC8135051), read verbatim:
- Burst definition, section B: "NOTE: Bursts must average >=0.5 seconds and have at least 4 phases (i.e., at least 3 baseline crossings); if shorter or fewer phases, they should be considered discharges."
- Highly epileptiform bursts, summary-of-changes table: "present if 2 or more epileptiform discharges (spikes or sharp waves) are seen within the majority (>50%) of bursts and occur at an average of 1 Hz or faster within a single burst (frequency is calculated as the inverse of the typical interpeak latency of consecutive epileptiform discharges within a single burst) OR if a rhythmic, potentially ictal-appearing pattern occurs at 1/s or faster within the majority (>50%) of bursts."
- Burst attenuation / burst suppression range 50 to 99 percent of the record, section B.
- Suppression ratio construction and its use for titrating anesthetic infusions to a burst-suppression target — Benedetti 2023, Amplitude-Focused Trends item 3 and Assessing Response to Antiseizure Medication and Infusion Titration.

## Judgment calls
- **The arithmetic is the item.** An interpeak latency of about 0.4 s inverts to about 2.5 Hz, which clears the 1 Hz threshold. Every distractor is a different way of getting that inversion wrong, or of applying the wrong numeric criterion (5 discharges, half the bursts, burst versus discharge).
- The vignette supplies burst duration (1.6 s), phase count (6), the fraction of bursts involved (about 80 percent) and the interpeak latency, so the answer is fully derivable from the stem; the reader is never asked to recall the criteria unaided, only to apply them.
- The stem intentionally gives numbers that clear every threshold comfortably rather than sitting on a boundary, so the item tests the rule and not judgement about borderline values.
- The suppression ratio is shown at 70 percent with the threshold set to 10 uV so the panel independently places the record in the ACNS burst-suppression band.
