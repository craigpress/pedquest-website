## Where each number came from
Srinivasakumar 2015. Population: randomized controlled trial of 69 neonates at or beyond 36 weeks with moderate or severe hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, randomized to treatment of electrographic seizures alone or treatment of clinical seizures; conventional EEG-video up to 96 h; MRI scored for injury severity; neurodevelopmental evaluation at 18-24 months.
- 35 of 69 (51 %) developed seizures: 15 in the electrographic-treatment group, 20 in the clinical group.
- Excluding infants with status epilepticus, median cumulative seizure burden 449 s (IQR 113-2070, n = 10) versus 2226 s (760-7654, n = 16), p = 0.02; the electrographic group had fewer seizures and shorter time to treatment.
- Increasing burden was associated with higher brain injury scores (p < 0.03) and lower performance across all three Bayley III domains (p = 0.03); 24 of 30 surviving infants with seizures were evaluated.

Glass 2019. Population: 534 neonates with acute symptomatic seizures in the Neonatal Seizure Registry.
- 66 % had an incomplete response to the initial loading dose of antiseizure medication; response did not differ by gestational age, sex, medication or dose.

## Judgment calls
- The correct answer is a management action anchored to a randomized trial plus a prospective multicenter registry, satisfying the requirement that management-style items be evidence-anchored.
- Option D (stop cooling) is again the harmful distractor and is named as such in its rationale.
- The stem describes electroclinical uncoupling after the first load: the movements stopped while electrographic seizures continued. The Variane cohort supports how common electrographic-only seizures are (213 of 296, 71.9 %) and is cited as supporting, not as the source of a stem number.
