## Where each fact came from
- Alpha-delta ratio construction ("a power ratio of faster alpha frequencies over slower delta frequencies ... averaged over time and displayed as a line graph") and the key sentence that sedation "would blunt alpha and delta amplitudes similarly, often without a noticeable change in the ratio" — Benedetti 2023, Frequency-Focused Trends, item 2, read from PMC10033183.
- "All patients with acute ischemic stroke (n = 5) had lower alpha and beta power and spectral edge frequency on injured compared with uninjured regions"; "All hemorrhagic stroke patients (n = 6) demonstrated negative association of total power with arterial blood pressure within injured regions"; total cohort 11 children, first 24 hours of continuous EEG — Appavu 2022 abstract.

## Judgment calls
- **Sample size stated openly.** n = 5 ischemic and n = 6 hemorrhagic out of 11; the explanation says the study is small and that pediatric validation of ischemia trends is limited, per STYLE_GUIDE section 2.
- The item claims only what the sources support: it does NOT assert an alpha-delta ratio threshold for ischemia, because no pediatric cutoff was found in a citable source.
- Distractor E (propofol beta) is a real pharmacological effect that is nonetheless irrelevant because beta lies outside both terms of the ratio; this is the discriminating distractor for readers who know propofol pharmacology but not the arithmetic.
- The aneurysm scenario is composite and generic; delayed cerebral ischemia is used only to motivate ischemia surveillance, and no outcome statistic is attached to it.
