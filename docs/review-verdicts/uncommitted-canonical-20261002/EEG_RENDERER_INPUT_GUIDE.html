How we author a synthetic EEG

We specify the background, add events and artifacts, and choose how to view the resulting recording. The inputs are a mixture of switches, numeric settings, choices, and timed events.

1 · Describe the case →Age, baseline activity, event timing, location and morphology.
2 · Generate voltages →Combine background activity, spatial fields, events and artifacts into electrode signals.
3 · Derive measurements →Calculate aEEG, power, frequency ratios, asymmetry, rhythmicity and suppression.
4 · Present the outputEEG pages, trend panels, or exported recordings with event information.

The central idea: an author describes the EEG features to generate. The renderer fills unspecified settings from presets and applies explicit overrides. A diagnosis or a free-text drug label does not, by itself, determine the waveform.

The controls a colleague would recognize

Context · choices + numbers

1. Whose EEG are we simulating?

  • Age group: neonate, infant, child, adolescent or adult.
  • Neonatal maturity: postmenstrual age; optionally hours of life or a younger modeled maturity.
  • Electrode coverage: standard 19-electrode or reduced neonatal array.

Age selects starting values for background frequency, amplitude and slow activity. Neonatal maturity also shapes burst timing and developmental transients.

Exact inputs

age_group · channels · background.pma_weeks (23–48) · hours_of_life · dysmature_pma_weeks (the last two are also inside background).

Background · choices + dials

2. What runs between events?

  • Background type: continuous, discontinuous, excessively discontinuous, tracé alternant, burst suppression, suppressed, low voltage or hypsarrhythmia.
  • Speed and size: dominant frequency, amplitude and proportion of slow activity.
  • Organization: posterior rhythm strength/field and anterior–posterior gradient.

These establish the baseline on which all events are superimposed. Increasing slow activity shifts the signal toward slower components; increasing amplitude makes the generated voltage larger.

Exact inputs

Inside background: type, dominant_hz (0.3–20 Hz), amplitude_uv, slow_fraction (0–1), pdr_gain (0–8), pdr_field (broad/focal), ap_gradient (present/absent). amplitude_reference selects display-referenced or referential amplitude; the current version-2 default is display-referenced. Requested amplitude is a synthesis target, not the height of every wave.

Continuity + location · dials

3. How continuous and symmetric?

  • Bursts: burst length, time between bursts, interval variability and residual interburst voltage.
  • Left–right relationship: burst synchrony, unilateral attenuation or slowing.
  • Regional emphasis: breach effect and a limit on unusually high electrode gains.

Longer interburst intervals create longer low-activity gaps. More unilateral attenuation reduces that side’s voltage. These changes can alter derived suppression and asymmetry trends.

Exact inputs

background.ibi_range_s, ibi_floor_uv, synchrony (0–1). Inside background.burst_suppression: burst_s, ibi_s, ibi_sigma, ibi_floor, and burst-complexity controls epileptiform_discharges, highly_epileptiform_fraction, interpeak_latency_s, phases.

background.asymmetry: side, attenuation_pct, slowing_hz, profile. background.breach: focus, gain, fast_gain. channel_gain_max is also a background setting. The authored IBI range describes the central 99% of a distribution, not hard minimum/maximum limits.

Physiology · switches + timed changes

4. State and developmental features

  • State: timed sleep, wake or arousal changes; optional term-neonatal state cycling.
  • Reactivity: baseline reactivity setting plus timed stimulation.
  • Developmental transients: delta brushes and individual neonatal graphoelements, with enable, rate and amplitude controls.
  • Pediatric variants: hypnagogic hypersynchrony, POSTS and posterior slow waves of youth.

Features are conditioned by the modeled age/state. A switch can enable a feature without forcing it into every visible page.

Exact inputs

background.state_cycle: term, reactivity; event types state_change and stimulation. background.delta_brushes: false, true (legacy model), or riding (discrete slow waves with fast activity).

background.graphoelements supports enabled, rate_per_min, amplitude_uv for occipital delta, temporal theta/alpha, sharp theta of prematurity (stop), frontal sharps, anterior slow activity, midline theta, delta brushes and sharp transients. background.variants contains the three named pediatric variant switches.

Epileptiform activity · scheduled events

5. What abnormal events occur?

  • Event family: isolated seizure, cluster, status, spasm/cluster, tonic seizure, brief rhythmic discharge, rhythmic/periodic pattern or sporadic discharge.
  • Where and when: onset time, duration, focus/region, recurrence and spread.
  • Shape and evolution: waveform family, starting/ending frequency and voltage, recruitment profile, postictal attenuation.
  • Accompanying muscle: none, modest or clinical.

A focal run can begin at one frequency, slow while growing in amplitude, and spread beyond its starting region. Sporadic discharges instead use a focus, discharge rate and spike/sharp/polyspike morphology.

Exact inputs

events[]: type, onset_min/start_min/at_min (depends on event), duration_s, onset_region, spread, morphology, muscle, postictal_attenuation_s. evolution: start_hz, end_hz, amplitude_start_uv, amplitude_end_uv, profile (sweep/recruit).

Clusters add interval/end timing and optionally duration_end_s. Rhythmic patterns add pattern, frequency_hz, amplitude_uv, run_duration_s, min_cycles, fluctuation, rate_jitter and modifiers. Sporadic discharges use focus, rate_per_h, aftergoing_slow. Spasms add decrement depth/duration and fast-activity controls.

Clinical course · scheduled changes

6. How does the recording change?

  • Sedation change: authored suppression target, beta boost, amplitude change and ramp time.
  • Attenuation: side, depth, duration and speed of onset; optionally spare slow activity relative to fast activity.
  • Temperature: starting/ending temperature and transition time.
  • Longer patterns: background amplitude/interburst changes over hours or cyclic alternating backgrounds.

These controls create a scripted time course. Sedation effects are authored simulation settings, not a conversion from medication dose.

Exact inputs

sedation_change: agent, direction, at_min, effect.suppression_ratio_target_pct, beta_boost, amplitude_pct, ramp_min (last three inside effect).

attenuation_transient: side, depth_pct, delta_depth_pct, ramp_min, duration_min. temperature_change: from_c, to_c, over_min. Background controls: amplitude_gain_at_h, ibi_floor_at_h, cape.

Artifacts · switches + scheduled events

7. What noncerebral activity is present?

  • Baseline contamination: blink rate/voltage and ECG voltage.
  • Timed artifacts: chewing EMG, patting, chest physiotherapy, ventilator, ECMO pump, electrode pop, 60 Hz interference, ECG, movement, sweat or eye blink.
  • Placement and strength: affected channels, onset, duration and low/medium/high intensity.

Artifacts are added to the voltage signals, so they can also change the calculated trends and illustrate false-positive detector behavior.

Exact inputs

background.blink_rate_per_min (0–60), blink_amplitude_uv (0–500), baseline_ecg_uv (0–30). events[].type: artifact with kind, at_min, duration_s, channels, intensity and model version.

Viewing + measurement · choices + dials

8. How do we look at it?

  • EEG page: montage, time window, sensitivity and filters.
  • Trends: which measurements to display, channel grouping, frequency/color ranges and suppression measurement thresholds.
  • Presentation: labels, annotations, clock, theme and image dimensions.

A lower µV/mm sensitivity setting makes the same voltage look taller. Montage and filters change the viewed trace; measurement thresholds change how a trend is calculated. Neither is the same as authoring a new cerebral event.

Exact inputs

montage: longitudinal bipolar, referential, average or neonatal reduced. at_min, window_s (5–30 s), sensitivity_uv_mm (1–100), filters.lf_hz, hf_hz, notch_hz. Trend inputs include panels, hemisphere_channels, style.suppression_threshold_uv and suppression_min_duration_s.

Some historical controls stored under style affect synthesis, including spindle_hz; the section name alone does not guarantee a cosmetic setting. annotations and clinical_correlate provide context without changing voltages.

Reproducibility and additional output controls

seed controls the repeatable random realization. Reproduction also requires the same normalized specification, renderer version, recording duration, sample rate and channel/reference settings. Changing duration can change the generated samples even at the same seed. spec_version selects defaults for omitted keys (current default: 2); sample_rate defaults to 256 samples/second.

Output kinds include EEG page, qEEG panel, aEEG and composite. aEEG adds pattern presets (CNV, DNV, BS, CLV, FT) and channel-pair choices. A source block is a separate real-recording input path. This guide describes the synthetic path.

Additional controls in candidate branches

PQW-109 adds agent-specific sedation levels (0–1 simulation effects) and complete neuromuscular blockade. PQW-110 adds mu, lambda, wickets, 14-or-6 positive bursts, RMTD, SREDA, a frontal arousal pattern pending classification, photic driving, hyperventilation buildup, and additional eye/pulse/glossokinetic artifacts. These are separate candidate implementations; the project handoff records clinical review as pending. They are not assumed available in the main renderer described above.
